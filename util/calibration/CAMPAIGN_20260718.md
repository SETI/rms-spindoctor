# Calibration campaign 20260718

Tracked record of the seed-20260718 sim-anchored calibration campaign:
what was fit, what the library cross-check measured against the
operator sidecars (with per-frame attribution), and what that means
for the CI gate.  Generated artifacts (row files, fit reports, the
cross-check tables themselves) live under `_work/calibration/`
(gitignored); this file preserves the decisions and the numbers that
outlive them.

## Campaign summary

Campaign seed 20260718, 4200 scenes per collection pass (600 per
family, 0 errors, ~7m19s per pass), collected on the staged
forward-model renderer with the full body surface / photometric truth
axes and, in the second stage, the full ring truth vocabulary.  Fit in
the README's order: per-technique alphas (`fit.py`), hand-written
YAML, re-collect, floors (`fit_floors.py`), tiers and final gate
(`fit_gates.py`).

Two stages, same seed:

1. **Full-truth-axis refit** (body families draw photometric law,
   opposition surge, limb relief, albedo texture, gouraud shading,
   pose scatter; disc family adds the giant-planet disc-texture /
   transit slice).  Highlights: BodyTerminatorNav's first sim-anchored
   fit (single-class, honest ~0.03-0.05 plateau, 4.32 px floor),
   BodyLimbNav's fitted visible-arc term and 2.61 px floor,
   BodyBlobNav's negative size alpha confirmed, the star-unique
   `predicted_snr` transform widened to /250.  Final gate and
   medium/low boundary 0.35.
2. **Ring-vocabulary refit** (ring family draws inclined projections,
   eccentric / m-mode / edge-wave orbits, planted per-feature orbit
   errors, non-navigable distractors; the disc transit slice casts
   moon shadows).  RingEdgeNav alpha0 1.998 -> 1.832,
   `total_edge_length_px` 1.137 -> 0.976, `per_edge_dt_rms_mean`
   -0.023 -> -0.069 (base rate 0.97 -> 0.90 with genuine failure mass
   in-cohort); RingAnnulusNav alpha0 0.744 -> 0.647, `ncc_peak`
   1.346 -> 1.168, `peak_to_runner_up_ratio` 1.147 -> 1.059,
   `annulus_count` 0.386 -> 0.593.  Floors re-solved to <= 0.13 px
   additional everywhere (no floor changed).  High tier
   `min_confidence` 0.55 -> 0.85: the planted ring orbit errors put
   tight-sigma confidently-wrong locks in the 0.55-0.80 band (success
   0.874-0.8999 against the 0.9 target), failure mass no ring
   diagnostic can see.

On convergence claims: scene draws are seed-deterministic and a
pass-1 technique's diagnostics and errors do not depend on any
confidence formula, so pass-over-pass alpha reproduction is
*structural* for the pass-1 techniques -- it verifies determinism,
not convergence.  The substantive convergence content is the
prior-dependent `StarRefineNav` cohort (re-fit and re-verified after
the pass-1 formulas changed), the floors re-solving to ~0 additional,
and the gate curves re-deriving to the shipped boundaries.  All three
held on the final pass.

Standing ensemble-gap evidence, strengthened by this campaign: the
`orbit_error_ringlet` scene still fuses to a confident high-tier ~3 px
wrong offset (0.89/high) even with planted orbit errors in the fit
cohort at scale and the high boundary raised on exactly that failure
mass.  The fit had its chance; closing this requires consuming the
declared `sigma_a_px` error bars in the covariance or the ensemble,
not a better sigmoid over diagnostics that cannot carry the signal.

## Calibration basis and scope

The disclosure that governs how every number in this record may be
read:

- **The empirical instrument chain was off.**  No campaign scene
  carries an `artifacts` block, so `instrument_defaults` (the emulated
  camera's tuned PSF kernel, detector chain, and artifact catalog) was
  never active.  The body and ring families render with no whole-scene
  PSF at all -- their limbs and ring edges are shaped by anti-aliasing
  only -- and the star families set `optics.psf: {match_navigator:
  true}`, the self-consistency floor form (a pure Gaussian equal to the
  navigator's own model).  No scene draws distortion, smear, or
  atmosphere; noise is Poisson plus a log-uniform read-noise draw
  (plus a stray-light slice on a quarter of the star-field family).
- **Single-instrument cohort.**  Every scene of all seven families
  emulates `coiss_nac`.  The fitted alphas, the model-error floors, and
  the tier boundaries are applied fleet-wide (Voyager, Galileo, LORRI,
  the WAC) from that one-camera cohort.
- **What the realism match does and does not vouch for.**  The
  simulator report's realism-match section quantifies the renderer
  *with the empirical chain on* against real cohorts.  That is a
  different configuration from the one this campaign rendered, so the
  realism evidence does not underwrite these coefficients.  What the
  campaign's scenes do carry is the surface / photometric / orbit truth
  vocabulary (limb relief, non-Lambert laws, albedo texture, mesh and
  pose error, planted orbit errors, catalog scatter, confounders) --
  real model error, honestly planted.  Two of those renderers were
  defective when this campaign ran: every limb-relief draw carved
  radial wedges into the disc (#625), and every mesh body rendered as
  banded shells (#626).  The planted relief and mesh error was
  therefore not the error the scenes describe; the refit on the
  corrected renderers is tracked in #793.
- **Consequence.**  The coefficients' meaning on real frames currently
  rests on the library cross-check below, not on the realism match;
  `confidence_provisional` stays true in every metadata product.  The
  standing recommendation is to re-run the campaign with
  `instrument_defaults` on across the families and refit, which is the
  change that would connect the realism evidence to the shipped values
  (see `critiques/archive/SIM_REALISM_CRITIQUE_2026-07-18.md`).
- **The generator has evolved since this campaign.**  After the
  recorded passes, `scene_gen.py`'s ring orbit-error slice changed to
  draw `declared_orbit_sigma` independently of the realized planted
  error (the campaign's rows were generated with a sigma of 0.8-1.5x
  the drawn error magnitude -- a correlation a real published
  uncertainty does not carry, harmless to the shipped fit because
  nothing consumed the declared sigma, but a leak for any future
  sigma-consuming refit).  Regenerating seed 20260718 with the current
  generator therefore will NOT reproduce the recorded rows bit for
  bit; the recorded artifacts remain this fit's evidence.

The one low->failed flip is a new post-#304 sidecar with no recorded
pre-refit measurement, so it was re-run alone through the cross-check

  best RANSAC transform has **1 inlier** against the minimum of 6 ->

  14.0 px > 4.0 px -> falls back to one-star; one-star brightness
  margin **0.65 mag** below the 1.5 mag floor -> spurious.

navigable, but the autonomous star gates cannot lock this frame) and
is independent of every value this campaign fit: the refit changed no
star-technique gate.  The flip is a pre-existing star-navigation gate
gap surfacing in the cross-check, tracked separately from
calibration.

## CI-gate consequence

The image-library sidecar expectations (`expected.status` /
`expected.confidence_tier`) were last reconciled against the
pre-refit pipeline (the post-Track-B behavior, #304).  This campaign
re-measures tiers wholesale -- 46/75 agreement, dominated by
high->medium under the 0.85 boundary and medium->low under the
2.61 px limb floor -- so:

- **The historical failure-set gate no longer applies as-is.**  The
  autonomous-library regression's recorded failure set predates the
  refit; branch comparisons against it will report calibration-driven
  tier re-measurements as new failures even though the offsets are
  unchanged (54/61 within slack, identical before and after the
  refit).
- **A post-merge sidecar re-ratchet is an operator decision.**  The
  expected tiers are operator judgments, never fit targets; whether
  to re-tier the sidecars to the recalibrated pipeline (and what to
  do with the six status-level disagreements above) is deliberately
  left to operator review rather than folded into this campaign.

## Transfer watch (proposed)

**Status: PROPOSAL awaiting operator adoption; nothing below is
armed.**  A calibration needs a pre-stated criterion for what would
falsify it on real frames -- otherwise every disagreement gets
attributed after the fact and nothing can ever count against the fit.
This section states one, grounded entirely in measurables that exist
today (`library_crosscheck.py` over the operator sidecars).  It
presumes the sidecar re-ratchet above has happened, so the expected
labels reflect the recalibrated pipeline and disagreement measures
drift rather than staleness.

Proposed criteria, each evaluated per cross-check run:

1. **Tier-conditional fit rates vs the sim-measured target.**  On
   frames with operator-verified offsets, the fraction of high-tier
   results within the high tier's error budget, and of medium-tier
   results within the medium budget, each compared against the 0.9
   success target the tier boundaries were derived to.  Proposed
   breach: a tier's measured rate below 0.75 with at least 20 frames
   in the tier (roughly the one-sided binomial 99% bound under a true
   0.9), or below 0.6 at any n of 10+.
2. **Fused 2-sigma coverage.**  The fraction of operator-verified
   offsets within 2x the fused sigma, against the 0.865 2D-Gaussian
   reference the floors were solved to.  Proposed breach: coverage
   below 0.70 with at least 40 usable frames.
3. **Cross-check agreement floor.**  Against the re-ratcheted
   sidecars: status agreement below 0.85 (2026-07-18 measured 69/75 =
   0.92), offset-within-slack below 0.80 (measured 54/61 = 0.89), or
   tier agreement below 0.80 on the re-ratcheted labels.

Proposed cadence: re-run the cross-check and evaluate all three after
every calibration refit, after any renderer change to a default stage,
and whenever a curation batch adds 10+ sidecars; record the numbers in
the campaign record of the run that triggered it.

Proposed breach handling: a breached criterion means the calibration
does not transfer as claimed.  The response is to record the breach,
investigate or refit, and re-state the criterion -- never to attribute
the disagreement away.  Per-frame attribution (as practiced above)
remains valuable forensics, but under this watch it explains breaches;
it does not excuse them.
