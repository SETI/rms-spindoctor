# Titan real-frame validation campaign, 2026-07-26

The record of the campaign that took the haze navigator from simulator-tuned
defaults to defaults set by real Cassini frames: what was run, what each
measurement said, which knob each measurement moved, and what is still open.

## 1. The cohort

`titan_images.csv` vendors 82 unique Cassini frames from a legacy annotated
test list (`/seti/all_repos/rms-csmithing/tests/titan_images.txt`, 87 lines,
83 entries, `N1484962455_2` listed twice). Every frame resolved to a
holdings path and every path exists on disk; every epoch falls inside the
Cassini tour window, so nothing was lost to SPICE coverage.

Flags were assigned by reading each freeform annotation:

| flag | rule | n |
|---|---|---|
| `rings_occluding` | the annotation says rings occlude, overlap, or sit too close | 11 |
| `moon_occluding` | a named moon is stated as occluding, or a second moon is called out as a problem | 10 |
| `high_phase` | the annotation says "high phase", or the filter series names a phase >= 140 deg | 7 |
| `known_bad` | "XXX BAD", "bad lines", "bad image entirely", or a legacy-method failure note ("Old - ...") | 7 |
| `off_edge` | Titan stated as off the edge, even "slightly" | 4 |
| `near_edge` | Titan stated as near the edge | 3 |
| `clean` | no defect named, or no annotation at all | 49 |

Two judgment calls are worth naming. A frame whose annotation only lists a
companion moon ("Titan Tethys") is `clean`: nothing is stated to be wrong
with it, and masking a sibling is behavior the method is supposed to have.
The two "Old - ..." frames are `known_bad` even though they describe a
LEGACY method failing rather than a defective image, because the annotator
put them in the problem block; the notes column preserves the distinction and
one of them, `N1484962455_2`, is discussed in section 6.

## 3. What each measurement decided

### 3.1 `max_residual_rms_px` stays at 2.0 (a lever the evidence refused)

The gate was the largest single cause of clean-frame refusal at baseline
(six frames), and the residual distribution invites raising it: a smooth
continuum from 0.29 to 3.03 px, then a clean gap to the gross failures at
8.99 px (an image the annotation calls "bad lines") and 57.9 px (a
ring-crossed frame). A cut inside that gap looks obviously right.

It is obviously wrong. At 3.5 px, `W1655808265_1` commits and disagrees
with its own independent star lock by 12.7 px and with its cross-filter
twin 99 seconds away by 8.7 px; `W1683615178_1` commits and disagrees with
its twin by 12.9 px. The frames sitting between 2 and 3 px of residual are
not noisier fits of the right answer, they are the wrong answer. The gate
keeps its shipped value.

### 3.2 `min_gradient_snr` 4.0 -> 8.0 (free on real frames)

The Phase-A review measured that 8% of simulated frames whose truth was
planted outside the search window locked confident-wrong at 4.0 and none
did at 8.0. The real-frame cost is negligible: of the 51 cohort frames whose haze
feature reached the technique in both runs, 45 keep an identical surviving-ray
count and six change -- 59 -> 37 and 59 -> 38 on a 166 deg phase pair,
59 -> 52, 59 -> 54, 56 -> 52, and 52 -> 51 -- every one still far above the
20-ray floor.  Residual RMS moves by at most 0.35 px on any frame.

### 3.3 The visibility hard-zero: per-axis, not scalar (a code change)

`titan_haze_reliability` forces reliability to zero when the envelope disc
dilated by the search window leaves the extended frame. The dilation used
the scalar search half-window, which is the LARGER of the two extfov
margins. On a Cassini NAC those margins are 50 rows against 140 columns,
and the extended frame is the detector plus those margins -- so dilating
the row axis by 140 asks the envelope to clear the detector by 90 px on
each side of the row axis, for no physical reason. Dilating each axis by
its own margin makes the test say exactly "the envelope clears the
detector", which is the visibility statement intended.

Measured: eleven `clean` frames were hard-zeroed at the predicted geometry;
eight of them have Titan entirely inside the detector. Three genuinely do
not (`N1635365617_1` and `N1637959164_1` have envelopes of 1075 and 1044 px
on a 1024 px detector; `W1548771820_1` overlaps the right edge) and stay
hard-zeroed.

This is a deviation from the plan's Section 2.5 wording, reconciled into the
plan in the same commit and marked pending operator ratification. The scalar
`window_px` is unchanged everywhere else: the search itself runs in the
rotated symmetry frame, where the plan's argument for a scalar holds.

### 3.4 `sector_half_angle_deg` 60 -> 80 (the largest accuracy change)

The constrained circle fit over a sunward sector determines the limb
position `d + R` far better than it separates the center shift `d` from the
radius `R`. For a uniform sector of half-angle `s` the correlation between
the two is `(sin s / s) / sqrt(0.5 + sin 2s / 4s)`: 0.984 at 60 deg and
0.942 at 80 deg, which is a factor of 3.5 in the variance the degeneracy
adds to `d`.

That matters because Titan's haze top is expected on prior physical
grounds to be wavelength-dependent, so the true `R` would differ between
filters, and a fit that cannot separate `R` from `d` puts part of that
difference into the offset; as shown below, this campaign could not itself
confirm the wavelength dependence against fit noise. The cohort measures
it directly, because it contains nine matched pairs taken 66 to 441 seconds
apart through different filters:

Every disagreement below is the rms of the PAIR difference -- two frames in
quadrature -- not a per-frame sigma; the per-frame figures appear in section
7 and are these divided by sqrt(2).

| channel | along-track pair-difference rms | at 60 deg | at 80 deg |
|---|---|---|---|
| star-anchored (tier a) | | 4.23 px (worst 10.58) | **1.50 px (worst 3.84)** |
| within-sequence, one filter (tier b) | | 0.85 px | **0.47 px** |
| cross-filter (tier c) | | 8.19 px (worst 17.63) | **2.81 px (worst 7.76)** |

The cross-track axis is unaffected (star-anchored pair rms 0.86 -> 0.99 px,
well inside its bound either way): the degeneracy is an along-track
property, exactly as the geometry says.

The single clearest sign that the change fixed the mechanism rather than
masking it is `N1647091889_1`: at 60 deg it committed at confidence 0.85
with an answer 10.6 px from its own independent star lock, and at 80 deg it
agrees with that lock to 0.75 px.

The fitted radii do NOT corroborate it, and the earlier draft of this record
claimed they did. Over the six red/violet pairs that commit on both sides,
the absolute fitted-radius difference spans 35.0-131.2 km at 60 deg and
11.8-97.8 km at 80 deg, with the spread about the mean essentially unchanged
(standard deviation of the signed difference 83.4 -> 68.1 km, of the absolute
difference 32.0 -> 30.0 km).  Three of the six pairs land within 66-68 km
at 80 deg where they ranged over 63-131 km at 60 deg; one is essentially
unchanged (35 -> 34 km) and the other two move
the other way (75 -> 12 km and 93 -> 98 km), so the tightening is not a
population effect and nothing here separates a physical haze-top difference
from fit noise.  The 4.23 -> 1.50 px star-anchored improvement and the
`N1647091889_1` recovery stand on their own; this does not add to them.

The cost is real and is recorded rather than smoothed: the simulated commit
rate falls from 65% to 59%. In the simulator the rendered haze limb IS the
circle being fitted, so the degeneracy has nothing to correct and the wider
sector only reaches further toward the terminator. The simulator's clean
accuracy bounds are unaffected (cross P95 0.169 -> 0.171 px against 1.0,
along P95 0.820 -> 0.841 px against 3.0).

### 3.5 `max_second_peak_ratio` stays at 0.90 (no real-frame evidence to move it)

The plan carried this forward because the simulator's low-phase side lobes
straddle the threshold while the recovered cross-track answer is exact. On
the real cohort the gate never fires: across the 63 frames that reach the
symmetry scan at all the strongest rival peak measured 0.688 (on
`N1504004058_2`), with a 95th percentile of 0.453.
The near-rotationally-symmetric low-phase disc
that produces the simulator's lobes is not a regime these frames reach, so
raising the threshold would spend a real false-lock margin to buy nothing
measurable.

### 3.6 Sigma floors stay where they are (real frames overrule the simulator)

The plan handed over both floors with simulator-implied targets: 0.08 px
cross and 0.63 px along, each the value that puts the reported sigma at the
campaign's measured rms. Real frames say the opposite of what those numbers
suggest.

| axis | reported sigma | simulator implies | star-anchored, per frame | within-sequence, per frame |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| cross | 0.36 px | 0.08 px floor | 0.70 px rms, worst pair 1.84 px (per frame 1.30) | 0.34 px rms |
| along | 1.02 px | 0.63 px floor | 1.06 px rms, worst pair 3.84 px (per frame 2.72) | 0.33 px rms |

The reported sigma is at or inside the real-frame spread on both axes, so
cutting either floor would report a tighter uncertainty than real frames
support. Both stay at their shipped values (0.30 and 1.00).

The along floor's known consequence is accepted deliberately:
`hypot(1.00, 0.20)` exceeds the 0.5 px `max_sigma_px` of the `high`
confidence tier, so a Titan-only frame caps at `medium`. For a single
quasi-circular feature whose along-track coordinate is degenerate with a
free radius, and whose along-track disagreement against an independent
star lock reaches 3.8 px, that is the honest statement.

The simulator's z-score band settles the same way, and the reason is worth
stating because the aggregate number looks alarming on its own. Over all
413 committed rows the along z-score standard deviation is 1.56 -- above
the [0.8, 1.25] band, where Phase D measured 0.51 below it. Restricted to
the rows the confidence spec calls confident it is 0.32, and at confidence
>= 0.7 it is 0.26. The whole of the excess is the small-disc tail the new
anchors reject: the reported sigma is if anything conservative on the
population the ensemble actually consumes. Raising the along floor to pull
the all-row statistic into the band would widen an already-conservative
uncertainty on trusted frames in order to cover frames the confidence spec
has already flagged. It was not done.

| population | n | cross z std | along z std | along max error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| all committed | 413 | 0.65 | 1.56 | 22.09 px |
| confidence >= 0.5 | 317 | 0.42 | 0.32 | 3.80 px |
| confidence >= 0.7 | 232 | 0.35 | 0.26 | 1.37 px |

### 3.7 `model_error_floor_px` stays at 0.2 (the fitter cannot see the floor's job)

`util/calibration/fit_floors.py`, run on 353 usable rows of the new `titan`
calibration family, solves the floor to **0.000 px**: the unfloored sigmas
already reach 0.997 2-sigma coverage against the 0.865 Gaussian target. That
is a statement about the simulator, where a rendered haze is exactly the
circle the fit assumes. The shipped 0.2 px is kept, which is strictly more
conservative than the solve, because on real frames the same coverage is
carried by model error the simulator does not express.

### 3.8 The apparent-size lever has no real-frame evidence

Phase D found every along-track error above 2 px came from a body under
40 px solid radius. The cohort cannot test it: the smallest apparent solid
radius in 82 real frames is about 79 px (200 px envelope) and the largest
about 424 px. `min_envelope_diameter_px` keeps its value, and the
small-disc regime remains a simulator-only finding.

## 4. Confidence anchors

Fitted by `util/titan_truth/fit_confidence.py` over the 413 committed rows
of the planted-truth campaign at the final configuration, and shipped as
that fit rounded to the nearest 0.05. The script runs both ways: `--propose`
prints the fit, and the default run reads the shipped anchors back out of
`config_510_techniques.yaml` and scores them against every acceptance check
below, so nothing here has to be taken on trust.

```bash
python util/titan_truth/fit_confidence.py _work/titan_truth/rows_final_config.jsonl \
    --propose --unbounded --real-rows _work/titan_cohort/final/rows.jsonl
# proposal: alpha0 -9.2777  symmetry_peak_score +2.7537
#           symmetry_valid_fraction +1.7328  arc_inlier_fraction +1.4899
#           envelope_diameter_px +5.5360  arc_residual_rms_px -2.5000
```

```
alpha0 -9.30   symmetry_peak_score +2.75   symmetry_valid_fraction +1.75
arc_inlier_fraction +1.50   envelope_diameter_px +5.55 (/160)
arc_residual_rms_px -2.50 (/3.0)
```

The generic calibration fitter (`util/calibration/fit.py`) is enrolled for
Titan and was run, but it cannot set these anchors, which is why a second
script exists. On the `titan` calibration family its label -- recovered
within 1 px -- has a base rate of 0.975, so every usable row lands in one
class, the fit puts all 353 of them in the top confidence bin, and the
coefficients it returns (0.67, 0.66, 0.77, 0.64, -0.07) discriminate
nothing. The question the anchors have to answer is not "is the bulk
accurate" but "are the few rows that are wrong by more than twice an axis
bound separated from the rest", so `fit_confidence.py` up-weights exactly
those rows.

Two properties of the fit are deliberate:

- The envelope diameter carries the largest weight because the campaign's
  failure mode is entirely a small-disc one: all seven rows wrong by more
  than twice an axis bound have envelopes of 73-140 px. Real frames run
  200-1075 px, where the term sits at its cap and the quality terms carry
  the discrimination.
- The residual alpha is BOUNDED at -2.5 in the fit's own configuration.
  Released, the same solve drives it to **-15.12** (reproduce with
  `--unbounded`), which is a near-hard gate at half a pixel of residual --
  correct in a simulator whose limb is a perfect circle, ruinous on real
  frames whose median residual is 1.1 px. That bound is where a
  sim-anchored calibration would otherwise have failed to transfer, and the
  `--real-rows` check is what confirms it did not.

Measured on the planted-truth campaign, by the verification run above:

- confident-wrong rows (confidence >= 0.5 and beyond twice an axis bound):
  **0 of 7**, the seven scoring 0.106 to 0.384;
- among the 317 rows the technique calls confident, cross P99 **0.518 px**
  against a 2.0 px limit and along P99 **0.992 px** against 6.0 px, with an
  along maximum of **3.802 px** -- so the criterion holds as an existence
  statement, not only as a percentile;
- mean absolute error by confidence quintile: 1.301, 0.330, 0.279, 0.289,
  0.192 px -- monotone within the noise of an 83-row bin.

Real-frame transfer, from the same run: 49 committed frames, confidence
0.527 to 0.875 (median 0.762), none below 0.5, so none falls under the 0.35
final-confidence gate and the tier assignment is unchanged. Confidence
remains provisional under the program-wide marker.

## 5. Acceptance

| criterion | bound | measured | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| clean frames producing an accepted result | >= 70% | 36/49 = **73.5%** | PASS |
| (a) + (b) consistency pairs passing per-axis 2-sigma | >= 90% | 10/12 = **83.3%** | FAIL |
| every clean-frame failure attributed | all | 13/13, all named (section 6) | PASS |
| adverse frames producing no confident-wrong lock | all | 18/27 gate or fail a named gate, 2 emit no feature at all, 7 commit; of those 7, four are corroborated by every witness bar `BodyBlobNav` (one of the four, `N1647092276_1`, also marginally fails its star anchor by 0.08 px), one has a real 3.3 px disc disagreement, two have no witness | PARTIAL |

**On the pair bound.** A per-axis 2-sigma test on two axes passes at
0.9545^2 = 91.1% when the covariance is exactly right, so a 90% bound sits
essentially at the ceiling of what an honest calibration can deliver. With
twelve pairs, 10 successes against an expectation of 0.911 has a binomial
probability of about 0.29 -- entirely consistent with correct calibration.
The two failures are `N1647092276_1`, which misses by 0.08 px on one axis,
and `N1647092996_1`, which misses by 1.09 px; neither is a lock error (the
offsets agree with the star anchor to 1.4 and 1.9 px), both are the
reported sigma being tight. Widening a floor would convert both to passes,
and section 3.6 is why that was not done. Reported, not loosened.

Two frames that this record's earlier draft listed here --
`W1629929515_1` and `N1585391759_1` -- commit in the shipped configuration
and are not refusals at all.

**Issue candidate A -- radial sampling reaches `r_env + pad + W` and the
search window is 140 px on a Cassini NAC**, so on a large Titan the outer
samples of a ray leave the frame and the whole ray is dropped even though
its limb is comfortably inside. Of the 81 rays an 80 deg half-sector at
2 deg spacing offers, `N1481452791_1` keeps 16 and `N1686939958_1` keeps 5.
The rule dropping a ray with any out-of-frame sample past the solid radius
is correct in principle; sizing the reach by the full search window rather
than by where the limb can actually be is what costs these frames.

**Issue candidate B -- the arc residual on a large real Titan is not the
same quantity as on a small one.** The refused frames run 569-847 px of
envelope against an accepted-population median of 518.4 px, and residual RMS
correlates with apparent size at +0.315 over the 49 committed frames. A
fixed 2.0 px cap is therefore a size-dependent gate. It cannot simply be
raised (section 3.1), but a size-relative form is worth measuring.

Twenty-seven frames carry an adverse flag. Eighteen either gate at hard-zero
reliability (14) or fail a named technique gate (4); two emit no feature at
all, both `known_bad` images that end `missing_data_dominant` before any
model runs; seven commit.

For the seven that commit, every independent witness on the frame, with the
per-axis 2-sigma verdict:

| frame | flag | witnesses (2-sigma verdict) |
|---|---|---|
| `N1634462210_1` | `known_bad` | limb pass, disc pass, blob pass -- all within 0.5 px |
| `N1701985208_1` | `moon_occluding` | star pass, limb pass, disc pass, blob pass |
| `N1634462549_1` | `moon_occluding` | limb pass, disc pass; **blob FAILS** (along -10.3 px) |
| `N1634463001_1` | `moon_occluding` | limb pass; **disc FAILS** (along +3.3 px vs its 2.75 px along-axis 2-sigma tolerance); **blob FAILS** (+81.9 px) |
| `N1647092276_1` | `moon_occluding` | limb pass; **star FAILS** by 0.08 px on one axis (offsets agree to 1.4 px); **blob FAILS** (along -7.8 px) |
| `W1676687378_1` | `rings_occluding` | **no independent witness**; the annotation names the E ring, which the 74490-140500 km occlusion band deliberately excludes, and the fit is clean (residual 0.28 px) |
| `N1484962455_2` | `known_bad` | **no independent witness** |

What that supports, stated at the strength the data carries rather than
rounded up: two of the seven are corroborated by every witness present, two
more by every witness except `BodyBlobNav` (one of those two,
`N1647092276_1`, also marginally fails its star anchor by 0.08 px on one
axis while agreeing to 1.4 px overall), one (`N1634463001_1`) has a
genuine 3.3 px along-track disc-correlator disagreement beyond its 2.75 px
along-axis 2-sigma tolerance, and two have nothing to check them against at all.

The acceptance criterion is not the 2-sigma test, though, it is the physical
one: wrong by more than twice a stated axis bound (2 px cross-track, 6 px
along-track) while calling itself confident. Under THAT screen every witness
on every one of the seven places the haze answer inside the bound -- except
`BodyBlobNav`, which exceeds it on three of them (along-track -10.3, -7.8,
and +81.9 px).

Those three are discounted, and the reason is measured rather than assumed.
(`BodyBlobNav` never runs on Titan itself -- no body model builds for Titan
-- so every blob witness here is a brightness centroid on a small co-visible
moon, and that centroid's own scatter is what is being measured.) Across
this run `BodyBlobNav` fails the 2-sigma test against the haze fit on **6 of
the 12** frames where both commit, including clean unoccluded ones, with a
worst reading 120 px from every other technique on its frame. A witness that
disagrees half the time on the easy population cannot settle the hard one.

The witness agreement rates are worth recording in full, because they say
something about the 2-sigma test itself rather than about this technique:
`BodyLimbNav` 11/12, `StarUniqueMatchNav` 7/9, `BodyBlobNav` 6/12,
`BodyDiscCorrelateNav` 3/7. Pairwise 2-sigma agreement is poor across the
fleet, not just against the haze fit -- which is the same observation
section 5 makes about the acceptance-2 bound, seen from the other side.

`N1484962455_2` is the single frame where the technique commits with no
other technique on the frame at all. Its annotation describes a LEGACY
method under-shooting along-track, which is consistent with the 26 px
along-track offset now recovered being right, but nothing in this campaign
can confirm that. It is seated in the operator review batch as frame 02 and
is the highest-value vote in it.

## 7. What the campaign proved about accuracy

The strongest per-frame evidence is the star anchor: a prior-free star
technique locking on the same frame measures the same scene-wide
translation, so the difference is the haze fit's error plus the star fit's.
Over nine such pairs, resolved onto the technique's own axes:

- cross-track: pair-difference rms 0.99 px, worst 1.84 px, implying about
  0.70 px per frame;
- along-track: pair-difference rms 1.50 px, worst 3.84 px, implying about
  1.06 px per frame.

(Every "rms" in this record is the rms of a PAIR difference -- two frames,
or a frame and its witness, in quadrature. The per-frame figure is that
divided by sqrt(2), and it is the one to compare against a stated bound.)

Against the plan's published target of <= 1 px cross-track and <= 3 px
along-track, real frames confirm the simulator's bound rather than merely
being consistent with it.

Repeat frames of one target through one filter agree to 0.34 px cross-track
and 0.33 px along-track per frame, and their fitted haze radii to 4 km --
the method's repeatability, with the filter held fixed.

Cross-filter pairs are the noisiest channel by construction and stay so:
2.88 px cross-track and 1.99 px along-track per frame. One pair,
`N1647091889_1` against `N1647092276_1`, disagrees by 11.5 px cross-track
while BOTH frames agree with their own star anchors to about 1 px -- the
spacecraft genuinely moved in the 387 seconds between them. Tier (c) cannot
separate a real pointing drift from an estimator error, which is why the
acceptance bound is stated over tiers (a) and (b).
